Leading teams through change

Four org transitions over five years: a restructure that taught me the system, a rebuild across two continents, a team built from scratch in New York, and a rebuild in Bangalore. Delivery held or accelerated through every one.

01 · FY21–23
where the system was built
Canada
The restructure that taught me the system. A team of eight lost its anchors overnight, and the way through became the loop I've run in every rebuild since.
1–5
All five steps were built here, starting with the hardest one: telling the org our model wasn't working.

I was leading a team of eight designers responsible for product design across all of Canada when Intuit shifted to a global operating model. Overnight, my designers lost their PM and product partners, who were reallocated to global segment teams. The team was talented and suddenly unanchored.

1
Name the fear before you redraw the org chart.

I started with the emotional reality before the org chart. I ran a hopes and fears exercise to surface what the change meant to each person, found the core anxiety was role clarity, and shared those themes with leaders across the organization.

2
Design the structure for what the next year requires.

Our first answer, shaped with global design leaders, was an embedded model. I mapped each of my designers into a global segment team aligned to Canadian priorities, negotiating the assignments directly with segment design leaders.

one team → embedded in segments → one owned pillar
3
Listen honestly for whether it is working.
The model wasn't working. I brought the evidence, and the org changed it.
It wasn't working. Checking in over the following quarters, designers told me they had become gap fillers for under-resourced teams: reactive, spread thin, handed solutions and asked for mockups. The model gave us autonomy but not purpose.
4
Fix what you built, even when you built it.

So I pushed to change it. Working with a design leadership partner, I brought my team's evidence to the global leadership table and made the case for a durable model instead: our team would own a lasting pillar of work for the developed markets. I defined the criteria we used to choose that pillar, including market similarity, offering maturity, and where cross-functional teams sat.

5
Move people deliberately toward what they are genuinely good at.

I matched designers to the new roles with a skills assessment I scored and validated with another design leader. I documented a transition plan for every individual, and held segment leaders to not over-committing my team while the shift landed.

That sequence became the system I ran in every transition after.
02 · FY24
the loudest step: design the structure
Singapore
Rebuilding across two continents. When the site closed, the commerce design capability had to be rebuilt without pausing the roadmap.
2
Design the structure for what the next year requires: split across North America and India, anchored by a leader on the ground.

When Intuit closed its Singapore site, the design capability behind our commerce work had to be rebuilt without pausing the roadmap.

I split the rebuild across North America and India: four designers in each, plus a design leader in India to anchor the new team long term. I staffed it through external hiring and two internal moves, one a promotion to staff designer, and sequenced hiring against knowledge transfer so domain expertise did not leave before the new team could absorb it.

Fully staffed in two quarters. A year later, all eight were still with the company, and two earned promotions during my tenure.

03 · FY25
the loudest step: move people deliberately
New York
Building a team and its culture from scratch, starting with moving a manager back to the craft he loved, then hiring the team around him.
5
Move people deliberately toward what they are genuinely good at. He thrived, and delivery accelerated.

When our triad doubled in New York, design was one manager and one staff designer. The manager wanted to return to the craft, so I worked with him to move into a staff role he was better suited for and genuinely wanted, then hired three designers in NYC and rebuilt the structure around that seniority mix. He thrived.

Once the team was hired, I created Delight Fest: I brought the entire triad, not just design, to New York to meet customers face to face, hear their stories, and bond as a new team around what Design for Delight means in practice. Culture was not a workshop after the fact. It was how the team formed.

delivery did not slip
It accelerated. Staffed in two quarters, and shipping before the team had even finished forming. The team went on to ship the platform reset that became the new sales tax application, a new filing experience and Intuit's first tax agent.
04 · FY26
the loudest step: fix what you built
Bangalore
The system, again. A third rebuild, run remotely, as the only leader outside the site.
4
Fix what you built, even when you built it. The third pass of the loop, this time from the other side of the world.

A year later, the organization co-located the entire triad in Bangalore, and I rebuilt the design team a third time, remotely, as the only leader outside the site.

The team that came out of this third rebuild kept shipping through the transition itself.

shipped by the rebuilt teams
A new inventory application, built around the operations of product-based businesses like manufacturers.
