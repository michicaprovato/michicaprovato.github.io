where design is heading · ai
The hardest design decisions in AI products aren’t about interface. They’re about authority: when should an agent act, when should it ask, and when should it hand off to a person entirely. I use two questions to decide. How reversible is the mistake, and does the person know something the AI doesn’t. Everything I’ve shipped in AI at Intuit came back to making that judgment explicit, so teams could build against it instead of relitigating it feature by feature.
building high-performing teams
Ten-plus teams, built and grown
Most recently I led a 15-person global design organization spanning ten-plus teams across commerce, sales tax, business tax, and platform. If you want to build a great product, first build a great team. I treat customer connection as what makes a team confident, not as a research activity. I’ve hired and onboarded dozens of designers, connected them to real customer problems, and coached them into confident contributors and leaders.

operational excellence at scale
Three systems I run
01 · talent and growth
Hire, onboard, coach
Hire for complementary strengths, onboard against a clear craft bar, and hold the rhythm: weekly 1:1s, monthly growth check-ins, real reviews twice a year.
02 · team structure
Organize for focus
Design paired tightly with PM and engineering, clear triad ownership and decision boundaries, and team shapes that change as the problem changes.
03 · planning and impact
Count in planning
Design in the room for quarterly and annual planning, with clear inputs into prioritization and impact tracked toward outcomes, not outputs.
Craft and vision are what I make. Culture and operations are what make them repeatable.
