case study 01
Amazon × Intuit
A first-of-its-kind integration. A working prototype that gave two companies something real to negotiate with, helping reestablish the contract between Intuit and Amazon.
the vision & partnership
The first time QuickBooks was ever embedded inside a third‑party platform
In 2024, Amazon and Intuit partnered to bring QuickBooks directly into Amazon Seller Central. I led the design vision that aligned both companies and shaped the foundation of the partnership.
customer problem
Manual processes blocking real growth
“I spend too much time manually aggregating data from various sources instead of focusing on growth. Limited financial reporting also makes it hard to gauge profitability accurately.”
what i led
A prototype, not a pitch deck
Two companies were negotiating with no shared, concrete reference point for what the partnership would look and feel like day to day. I directed the end-to-end vision, and my team built a working prototype of QuickBooks inside Seller Central, with profitability at a glance, cash flow, and tax estimates, so both sides were negotiating against something real instead of a slide.
01 · in the room
It became a reference point in the negotiation.
Leadership on both sides pointed to it as the picture of what the partnership could be. In our VP of Product’s words: “The E2E provocation your team created, with your strong oversight, was absolutely critical to landing the partnership contract with Amazon.”
02 · after the deal
A repeatable model, not a one‑off.
It became the reference point for how the two companies think about future partnerships, and set the direction for what embedded financial experiences could look like next.
03 · in the news
Announced December 2024.
QuickBooks was announced as Amazon’s preferred partner for financial management inside Seller Central, with a rollout to millions of US sellers now underway, covered by CNBC and the trade press.

case study 02
AI for tax
Designing Intuit’s first AI tax agent. A story about deciding when an AI should act on its own, when it should ask for help, and when it shouldn’t answer at all.
quickbooks.intuit.com/business-tax-ai
customer problem · tax anxiety
“I want to pay as little tax as possible, but I worry I’m missing deductions. I don’t always know which expenses count, and I sometimes forget expenses paid outside of QuickBooks. It leaves me feeling anxious that I’m leaving money behind.”
a small business owner, in research
job to be done
When preparing taxes, business owners want to confidently capture all eligible deductions so they don’t miss savings or feel uncertain.
what we built
The Deduction Optimizer
The agent surfaces deductions business owners commonly miss, in language modeled on CPA tax worksheets, at the moment they’re reviewing what they owe. Not a generic list, but a set of prompts built around the specific expenses this business already has in QuickBooks.
designing for calibrated trust
An autonomy spectrum, not a single mode
I decided where the agent sat on a spectrum from full autonomy to full human control, and made that choice visible instead of buried in a setting.
full autonomy
01
Autopilot
Surfaces a deduction with its reasoning, in language a CPA would recognize. The agent acts, the person reviews after the fact.
shared control
02
Co‑pilot
The agent handles the routine parts, but asks before anything consequential goes through. The door to a human sits next to the action itself.
full human control
03
Suggest only
Some moments aren’t the agent’s to resolve. It routes straight to a person instead of guessing with confidence it hasn’t earned.

case study 03
Platform transformation
How a jobs-to-be-done architecture unified the QuickBooks platform, and what happens when the right architecture ships at the wrong moment.
act one · the constraint
The right vision. The wrong conditions.
QuickBooks had grown to 20+ apps buried in a monolithic nav. We restructured the platform around the jobs customers need to do, but launched ahead of a pricing change, on a compressed timeline, with no change-management plan. New customers thrived; existing customers and accountants felt disrupted.
act two · the pivot
We stopped. We listened. We rebuilt.
Over two quarters we shifted from acquisition to existing-customer confidence, rebuilding the 8-year-old Sales Tax experience around deadlines and filing confidence. The architecture held, and it made direct filing (case study 04) possible.
looking back
Migration is a design problem, not a cutover, and change management belongs to design, not just comms. A sound structure still fails if you ship it into the wrong moment.

case study 05
Invoicing, without the work
Eliminating the manual step between a customer request and a paid invoice: AI reducing friction for owners who can’t afford to lose time.
project alaska · from hackathon to ai flagship
Forward an email. Review a draft invoice.
Small businesses run on email, but every invoice still had to be retyped into QuickBooks. What began as a Global Engineering Days proof of concept became a core launch use case for Intuit Assist, automatically extracting customer, items, quantities, and amounts into a ready-to-review invoice. The innovation led to a patent filing.
value · time savings
All customers immediately recognized the value. Reviewing an invoice is far easier than creating one from scratch.
trust · assistive control
The side-by-side email view increased confidence; customers adopted it even knowing accuracy would be imperfect.
complexity · real-world variability
Highly variable email formats, but adoption held, provided mismatches were clearly flagged and easy to correct.

Canada payroll journey
How customer obsession became the team’s compass, turning a non-compliant product into a Scott Cook Innovation Award winner.
the challenge
Get full triad teams closer to the customer.
In 2018, QuickBooks Online Payroll Canada wasn’t fully compliant with Canadian regulations, and recommendation scores were 25 among small businesses and just 1 among accountants. I led the empathy practice: panels, follow-me-homes, journey mapping, and rapid prototyping at QuickBooks Connect, then coached teams to run it themselves. Vacation pay alone now saves owners an hour every pay run.
